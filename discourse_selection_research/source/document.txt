An Economic Model of Discourse Participation

*Why can a position appear more prevalent than it actually is?*

*Why can a debate persist even when nobody becomes more extreme?*

*Why might today's participants reflect yesterday's incentives?*

Model

Belief-related differences in participation, persistence and amplification can distort apparent prevalence. Experts answering questions can also be overrepresented; selection need not reduce quality.

If satisfied participants exit sooner than motivated disputants, debate can persist without anyone becoming more extreme. This requires selective exit, not merely continuing disagreement.

Fixed differences in entry and retention already generate selection. If approval, rejection or expression costs change later participation probabilities, today's participants also reflect yesterday's incentives.

Kirchner writes in Via productiva (2022), “The people with the most relevant insight are probably busy doing stuff.” Valuable outside work may deter public advice while selective private advice remains worthwhile.

For person i, day t and advice channel k (public or private), let Bᵢₖ(t) be expected benefit, cᵢ(t) the opportunity cost per hour, and τᵢₖ(t) the hours required. Net benefit, measured in utility units, is:

Vᵢₖ(t) = Bᵢₖ(t) − cᵢ(t)τᵢₖ(t).

Choose the highest net benefit plus an option-specific random shock among public advice, private advice and other work (reference benefit zero). Expertise may increase c, but also B or efficiency; its effect on public participation is ambiguous.

Fit only a reduced-form public-return process after first contribution. Aᵢ(t)=1 denotes a comment in the thread on day t since release; otherwise Aᵢ(t)=0. Define σ(z)=1/(1+exp(−z)), α as common baseline net benefit, δ as daily decline, and ρ as association with yesterday's activity. A standard logistic net-utility shock gives:

Pr[Aᵢ(t)=1 | history] = σ[α − δt + ρAᵢ(t−1)].

After an inactive day, return probability is q(t)=σ(α−δt); after an active day, continuation probability is s(t)=σ(α−δt+ρ). Positive ρ may reflect feedback, habit or persistent individual differences; it does not identify causation.

An unestimated extension lets commitments and feedback change eᵢ(t), resistance to belief revision. Entrenchment may affect later updating and participation. The fitted α does not separate benefits, opportunity costs or channel choice.

Data

The LessWrong release thread for Alignment Faking in Large Language Models contains 53 comments from 27 accounts during December 18, 2024–January 1, 2025 UTC (snapshot: November 19, 2026). Starting the day after each account's first contribution gives 352 account-days, seven with comments. Multiple same-day comments count once. Maximum likelihood conditions on initial arrivals; it does not predict them. Never-posting experts, opportunity costs, private advice and private beliefs are unobserved; deleted comments cannot be recovered.

Results

Compare three specifications: constant participation probability; declining net benefit; and declining benefit plus previous-day activity. Their AIC scores, which penalize model complexity, are 70.7, 40.0 and 41.9 respectively. Lower is better. The two-parameter decline model is preferred, with α=1.46 and δ=1.67. Its expected returning-account counts on December 19–21 are 3.14, 2.94 and 0.74, versus 3, 3 and 1 observed.
